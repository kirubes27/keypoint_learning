# MOE - 08.06.2026

## Background & motivation

The project asks whether useful keypoint coordinates can be selected by the requirement that rigid transformations follow a simple, compositional law. **This week, the focus shifted from whether the operator can learn the rotation to whether the resulting keypoints form a genuinely distinct and stable representation.**

## Gap & goal

Earlier experiments showed predictive, mostly object-localized keypoints, but did not establish that all keypoints represent different object parts or remain attached to those parts. **The immediate goal is therefore to identify a configuration that combines correct shared dynamics with keypoint distinctness and low object-relative sliding over a full 360-degree rotation.**

## Approach

**I completed a 324-configuration `shared_affine` sweep on the engineers' hammer and evaluated operator geometry, localization, compositionality, heatmap quality, participation, sliding, and observed keypoint separation. `L_act` was set to zero because displacement-only direction classification is ill-posed on a closed orbit; it will instead be tested separately on a limited arc. I also replaced dispersion-weight-based selection with measurements of the actual keypoint locations.**

## Results

Cumulatively, the experiments show that a simple shared operator can learn non-trivial rotational dynamics while keeping most keypoints on the object, but representation degeneracy remains unresolved.

**Across the new sweep, all runs learned the correct rotation direction; the median angle was 6.15 degrees, 322/324 runs were within 0.5 degrees of the intended 6-degree step, and median on-object localization was approximately 80%.**

**For Task 80, the inverse and cycle objectives are also working as intended. The forward and inverse one-step errors are similar (`0.0020` and `0.0022`), the inverse is well-conditioned (`condition number = 1.006`), and the cycle loss is approximately zero (`3.9e-6`). Across the sweep, stronger inverse/cycle weights generally improve invertibility, orthogonality, and full-orbit closure, although they do not substantially improve intermediate `W^10` error or keypoint distinctness. These losses therefore help produce a rotation-like operator but do not solve the representation problem. Because Task 80 uses both losses, it is an assisted result rather than evidence that the rotation emerges from prediction alone.**

**Here, `L_cycle` means applying the learned forward and inverse operators and returning to the same keypoints after one step; it is different from the `W^60` closed-orbit diagnostic, which repeatedly applies the forward operator over the complete rotation. Dispersion is necessary for the strongest representations but still permits local duplicate pairs. Low entropy weight produces mostly sharp heatmaps but does not prevent a dead channel, while smoothness has little measurable effect across the tested range. `L_act` and `L_loc` are both off by design in this experiment.**

**The ablations confirm that the learned structure is non-trivial: replacing Task 80's operator with the identity increases error by `1.90x`, while random operators, shuffled keypoints, and random keypoints increase it by approximately `84x`, `105x`, and `256x`. The learned operator itself is close to rigid rotation (`5.87` degrees, singular values `0.993-1.000`, spectral radius `0.996`).**

**Task 80 (`ent=0.01`, `disp=0.1`, `inv=0.5`, `cycle=0.5`, `smooth=0.001`) is the provisional leading configuration. However, its representation is not yet satisfactory: the current audit finds approximately 22% near-duplicate keypoints, one dead heatmap channel, 40% sliding keypoints, and only 50% clean keypoints. The duplicate analysis currently uses one frame and must be repeated across several poses.**

**I also found that the previous `k10/k1 < 5` gate is dominated by its small one-step denominator and should be removed. I will instead evaluate absolute prediction error at several intermediate transformation horizons, which can also be applied to limited yaw and pitch ranges. For the present full-360 roll experiment, I will additionally summarize the complete `W^1...W^60` trajectory and closed-orbit error. Closed-orbit evaluation will remain a roll-specific diagnostic rather than a general criterion for every transformation.**

**A note on the compositionality error bars: they are within-sequence s.e.m. over overlapping start-frame windows of a single seed, so they look very tight but understate the real uncertainty (the across-phase spread is large, and the windows are not independent). The reproducibility error bars that matter will come from s.e.m. across the planned multi-seed reruns.**

**Because this is a large sweep, I have not yet manually reviewed every configuration in depth. These are the clearest current failure modes from the quantitative ranking and visual inspection, rather than final conclusions.**

## Significance & broader impact

**The current results reveal an important distinction between learning a transformation and learning a useful representation. The model can recover an accurate shared rotation operator even when some keypoints overlap, collapse, or slide across the object. Low prediction error and correct operator geometry are therefore not sufficient evidence that meaningful object parts have emerged. A successful account must show both that the transformation is simple and compositional in keypoint space, and that the coordinates are distinct, object-attached, and stable across poses. Establishing both properties would provide stronger evidence that transformation prediction can select interpretable object structure, rather than merely exploiting redundant coordinates. This is an essential validation step before extending the framework to multiple operators, general 3D transformations, or less controlled domains such as faces.**
